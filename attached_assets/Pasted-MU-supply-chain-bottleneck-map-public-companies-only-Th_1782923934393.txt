MU supply-chain bottleneck map — public companies only

This is not “every company that could theoretically sell into semis.” This is the investable MU-critical map: companies tied to Micron directly, or public companies controlling inputs Micron explicitly says matter: advanced equipment, wafers, chemicals, gases, photoresists, substrates, PCBs, reticle blanks, assembly/test, external manufacturing, construction, utilities, and facilities. Micron’s own 10-K says certain materials/components/services are limited-source or sole-source, and names the exact categories that can constrain shipments: chemicals, silicon wafers, gases, photoresists, semiconductors, substrates, lead frames, PCBs, targets, reticle glass blanks, outsourced assembly/test, contract manufacturers, logistics, utilities, and advanced equipment.

Scoring: 90–100 = true chokepoint / scarce / highest leverage to MU capex or HBM. 80–89 = critical supplier layer. 70–79 = important bottleneck with competition. 60–69 = useful but less scarce. Below 60 = mostly infrastructure/IT/logistics, still relevant but not where the alpha usually hides.

Micron is in record expansion mode: FQ3 2026 capex was $7.1B, Q4 guide implied continued huge spend, and Micron highlighted HBM4 high-volume shipments plus HBM4E volume production expected in calendar 2027. Reuters also reported $22B of strategic customer commitments and tight memory conditions expected beyond calendar 2027, so the bottleneck stack matters now — not “someday.”

Best asymmetric smaller/mid-cap angles

The mega-cap equipment names are the obvious toll roads. The less obvious “could still surprise” names are:

Hanmi Semiconductor — probably the cleanest HBM packaging bottleneck. Direct MU supplier award, tied to assembly/test capital equipment, and the TC-bonder narrative is exactly where HBM scaling gets ugly. This is the kind of thing the market eventually treats like “picks and shovels with a chokehold.”

Siltronic / GlobalWafers / SUMCO — wafer supply is boring until everyone needs 300mm capacity at the same time. Siltronic has direct MU award evidence.

Tripod / SIMMTECH / Unimicron / Kinsus / Nan Ya PCB — substrate/PCB layer. Less sexy than HBM, but HBM packages and memory modules don’t ship on vibes. They need boards/substrates.

Phison / Montage / Silicon Motion / Rambus — downstream controller/module-chip exposure. More cyclical and less “hard chokepoint” than tools/materials, but potentially more asymmetric than ASML/AMAT if memory stays hot.

FormFactor / Neosem / Cohu / Onto / Camtek — test/metrology small/mid-cap basket. This is where yield pain becomes revenue.

Bias

Bullish on the MU supplier ecosystem, but not equally bullish on every name. The best setup is not “buy everything semicap.” It is HBM packaging + lithography/etch/metrology + wafers/materials + substrates/test. That’s where scarcity meets spend.

Macro overlay

Risk-on for AI memory capex, but cyclical-risk high. Micron’s customer commitments and HBM/advanced DRAM demand are screaming “hot table,” but memory is still memory: when supply finally catches up, pricing power can compress fast. Reuters explicitly flagged that the bull case depends on tightness staying tight.

Technical summary

No chart trade here yet. For entries, I would only buy individual suppliers if they are in Stage 2 / continuation breakout or pulling back constructively to rising key moving averages. No falling knives, no “it’s a bottleneck so I can ignore the chart.” That’s how portfolios get turned into confetti.

Best setup

Highest-quality basket: ASML, LRCX, AMAT, KLAC, TEL, Hanmi, DISCO, Siltronic, Shin-Etsu, Entegris, SCREEN, Fujifilm, TOK, Tripod, Phison, Montage, Powertech.

Most explosive research candidates: Hanmi, Neosem, Tripod, SIMMTECH, Montage, Phison, GlobalWafers, FormFactor, Onto, Camtek.

Invalidation

If Micron or SK Hynix starts signaling HBM supply normalization, capex digestion, lower tool intensity, or substrate/test bottleneck easing, reduce the long-tail suppliers first. The big equipment names can survive cycles; the smaller suppliers get body-slammed when orders pause.

Social sentiment overlay

Sentiment is hot-to-euphoric around MU/HBM right now. That helps momentum, but crowding risk is rising. The less crowded edge is not MU itself anymore; it’s the suppliers the market still treats like boring plumbing.

Trade grade

Theme grade: A- / 84. Strong macro alignment, strong catalyst support, excellent bottleneck structure. Not A+ because a lot of obvious names have already rerated and memory cyclicality can turn violent.

Position size

For a diversified supplier basket: 6–10% total portfolio max unless you already own heavy semis/memory. For a single small/mid-cap supplier: 0.5–1.5% risk-defined position, depending on chart and liquidity. Do not put five “different” HBM suppliers in the portfolio and pretend correlation does not exist. It’s one trade wearing five costumes.

Portfolio risk check

If you already own MU, NVDA, AVGO, AMD, WDC/SNDK, semicap, or AI infra, this basket is highly correlated. Treat the whole thing as one AI-memory-capex exposure bucket.

Biggest risk to thesis

The biggest risk is supply catches up faster than expected or customer qualification shifts away from premium HBM economics. Second biggest: the public supplier you buy is real but already priced like the bottleneck has been discovered by everyone with a Bloomberg terminal and three coffees.
