Meta supply-chain thesis

Meta is no longer “just” an ad platform. The real bottleneck stack is compute → packaging/HBM → networking/optics → power/cooling → data-center execution. Meta raised 2026 capex guidance to $125B–$145B, citing higher component pricing and future data-center capacity costs. That tells you exactly where the stress is.

Meta has confirmed it is using NVIDIA, AMD, and its own MTIA custom silicon, with GB200/GB300-class racks driving absurd power density and liquid-cooling needs. AMD now has a multi-year 6GW Instinct GPU agreement with Meta, while Broadcom has a direct MTIA custom silicon + packaging + networking partnership with an initial >1GW deployment and multi-GW roadmap.

This is exactly the “work backward from hyperscaler capex into physical bottlenecks” framework: map the end customer, find the scarce chokepoints, then identify public companies with the highest replacement difficulty. The power-law reason this matters: the best returns often come from a small number of companies sitting at the intersection of possibility, adoption, and market misunderstanding.

Layer-by-layer map
1. AI compute accelerators and custom silicon

Meta’s confirmed stack: NVIDIA Blackwell/GB200/GB300, AMD Instinct/EPYC, and MTIA custom silicon. Meta specifically says it uses AMD, NVIDIA, and custom silicon; its GB200 pod shows 72 Blackwell GPUs and extreme power/thermal demand.

Priority	Public companies
Core	AVGO, AMD, NVDA, TSM, SK hynix, MU, Samsung
Secondary	ARM, MRVL, INTC, QCOM
Upstream semi tools	ASML, AMAT, LRCX, KLAC, Tokyo Electron / 8035.T, ASM International / ASMI.AS, BESI, DISCO / 6146.T, Advantest / 6857.T, Teradyne / TER, Camtek / CAMT

Best bottleneck read: Broadcom is the cleanest Meta-specific custom silicon play. AMD is the cleanest confirmed non-NVIDIA accelerator expansion. NVIDIA is still the king, but Meta is clearly trying not to be trapped under one GPU vendor forever.

2. Foundry, CoWoS, substrates, OSAT

This is the “you can design the chip, but can you package the damn thing?” layer. TSMC is the central bottleneck because NVIDIA, AMD, and Broadcom custom silicon all need advanced nodes and advanced packaging. CoWoS remains the key wafer-level packaging approach for the largest AI processors.

Investing read

Bias: Structurally bullish on the Meta AI infrastructure supply chain, but many obvious names are already crowded.

Macro overlay: Neutral-to-risk-on for AI infrastructure capex; risk-off if rates spike, credit spreads widen, or AI capex gets questioned. Power/data-center policy remains a tailwind, with FERC pushing grids to overhaul large-load connection rules for data centers.

Best setup type: Do not chase every supplier. The best hunting ground is smaller public bottleneck names with real revenue, accelerating backlog/orders, and direct exposure to AI data-center power/optics/cooling.

Most important public names by actual Meta exposure: AVGO, AMD, NVDA, ANET, GLW, ETR, NEE, WMB, CRWV, NBIS, GOOGL, QCOM, EssilorLuxottica, Goertek.

Biggest risk to thesis: AI capex digestion. If Meta slows spending, delays projects, or investors force a “show me ROI” pivot, the high-multiple suppliers get hit first. The other risk is substitution: Meta is actively diversifying away from single-vendor dependence, which is bullish for AMD/Broadcom but caps infinite NVIDIA-style extrapolation.
