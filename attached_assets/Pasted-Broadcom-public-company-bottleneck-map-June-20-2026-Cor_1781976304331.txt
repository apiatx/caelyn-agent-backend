Broadcom public-company bottleneck map — June 20, 2026

Core truth: Broadcom’s constraint is not “can it design chips?” It is reserved leading-edge TSMC capacity + CoWoS/advanced packaging + HBM + ABF substrates/PCBs + lasers/optical components + OSAT/test execution. Broadcom’s FY2025 10-K says it outsources most manufacturing, names TSMC as its key front-end foundry, names TSMC, ASE, Foxconn, Amkor, and Siliconware/SPIL for assembly/test, and says ~95% of wafers manufactured by its contract manufacturers in FY2025 came from TSMC. That is the choke point. Not subtle. Not cute. It’s TSMC with a crown and everyone else begging for wafers.

Broadcom’s AI exposure is now big enough that these constraints matter immediately: Q2 FY2026 revenue was $22.2B, AI semiconductor revenue was $10.8B, +143% YoY, and Broadcom guided Q3 AI semiconductor revenue to $16.0B, +200%+ YoY, driven by custom AI accelerators and AI networking.

This is a bottleneck score, not a buy rating. It ranks how hard the layer is to replace, how long qualification takes, how directly it blocks Broadcom shipments, and whether scarcity can create pricing power. That fits your power-law/bottleneck lens: the market’s real upside usually concentrates in a small number of structural winners, not a giant basket of “kinda related” names.

Highest-conviction bottleneck clusters
1. TSMC is the single biggest Broadcom bottleneck

Broadcom can design monster XPUs all day. If TSMC leading-edge + CoWoS allocation is tight, shipments get throttled. Broadcom’s own disclosure says TSMC produced ~95% of its CM wafers in FY2025, and Reuters reported Broadcom calling TSMC capacity a 2026 choke point.

Best public exposure: TSM.

2. HBM is the second choke

Broadcom’s 3.5D platform needs HBM stacks, and industry-wide AI chip scaling is heavily constrained by HBM and CoWoS, not just logic dies.

Best public exposure: SK hynix, Micron, Samsung.

3. ABF substrates and optical PCBs are the under-owned bottleneck

Broadcom specifically called optical-transceiver PCBs an unexpected bottleneck, with lead times moving from roughly six weeks to six months.

Best public exposure: Ibiden, Unimicron, Nan Ya PCB, Kinsus, AT&S, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, TTM.

4. Optical/laser supply is the smaller-player hunting ground

Broadcom flagged laser supply constraints; the public small/mid-cap angle lives in COHR, LITE, FN, AXTI, IQE, AAOI, plus Asian optical players like Eoptolink and Innolight. This is where the basket can get explosive, but also where garbage entries get punished. No hero ball without charts.

5. EDA/test/metrology are “boring toll roads”

Synopsys, Cadence, Siemens EDA, Advantest, Teradyne, FormFactor, Camtek, Onto and KLA are less sexy than “AI chip winner,” but they are buried in every serious AI-chip ramp.

Final read

For Broadcom, the most investable bottleneck map is:

TSMC / CoWoS → HBM → ABF substrates/PCBs → OSAT/test → lasers/optics → EDA/IP → rack/ODM/power.

The asymmetric hunt is not “buy everything Broadcom-adjacent.” It is finding the names where Broadcom AI demand collides with scarce capacity, long qualification cycles, and still-not-insane valuation. Right now, the best hunting zones are HBM, substrates/PCBs, optical lasers/components, advanced packaging/test, and select EMS/ODM infrastructure.
