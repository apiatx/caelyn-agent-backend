### Layer-by-Layer Bottleneck Map

#### 1. Starlink terminal silicon and phased-array electronics

Core public winner: STMicroelectronics. This is the highest-confidence public SpaceX supplier because ST explicitly says its products are used across Starlink broadband satellites, direct-to-cell satellites, ground infrastructure, and user terminals. Reuters also reported ST has shipped over 5B RF antenna chips to SpaceX over the decade-long relationship.

Bottleneck logic: phased-array RF chips, secure chips, GNSS, MCUs, and high-volume manufacturing are hard to swap once designed in. That's sticky supplier DNA.

#### 2. Starlink routers, PCBs, solder, and terminal electronics

Core public cluster: Wistron NeWeb, Chin-Poon, Universal Microwave, Shenmao. Reuters directly tied these Taiwanese suppliers to SpaceX/Starlink production and noted SpaceX has pushed some suppliers to move manufacturing outside Taiwan for geopolitical-risk reduction.

Smaller-player watchlist: Compeq, Tong Hsing, Kinpo, Meiko. These are not as cleanly confirmed, but they are exactly the type of Taiwan/Japan electronics names that could get speculative rerates when SpaceX supplier chatter heats up.

#### 3. Starlink gateway / satellite RF backhaul

Core public winner: Filtronic. This is the most interesting small-cap bottleneck after ST. SpaceX did not just place orders; it took warrants and established a strategic partnership. That screams "critical enough to lock in."

Secondary: Universal Microwave Technology for satellite RF/microwave components. Less pure than Filtronic, but still high-signal.

#### 4. Launch cadence: liquid oxygen, nitrogen, and launch-site industrial gas

Core public winner: Linde. If Starship launch cadence scales, propellant infrastructure becomes a physical bottleneck. Linde has direct launch-site relevance in Texas and Florida and has built/expanded oxygen/nitrogen capacity near SpaceX operations.

Bottleneck logic: rockets do not launch on vibes. They launch on propellant, logistics, permitting, and ground systems.

#### 5. Raptor / Starship manufacturing and additive manufacturing

Core public supplier: Velo3D. SpaceX licensed Velo3D's additive-manufacturing technology, and Velo3D lists SpaceX as a customer. The strategic importance is real; the equity quality is the ugly part.

Materials proxies: Materion, Howmet, Carpenter, ATI, Outokumpu. These are category-critical aerospace/materials names, but I would not treat them as confirmed SpaceX suppliers unless a filing, customer slide, or credible report specifically names SpaceX.

#### 6. Direct-to-cell / spectrum layer

Core public asset supplier: EchoStar. SpaceX acquiring spectrum from EchoStar is a critical infrastructure step for direct-to-device Starlink. This is less "supplier margin upside" and more "strategic asset transfer."

Commercial partner: T-Mobile. Important for distribution and network integration, but not the asymmetric small-cap bottleneck you're hunting.

#### 7. Orbital AI / compute / TeraFab semiconductor stack

This is the future bottleneck layer, not the clean current supplier layer. SpaceX filings reportedly say it still expects to source significant compute hardware from third-party suppliers, while the Tesla/SpaceX/TeraFab concept could eventually pull more chip production in-house.

Public names to monitor: Intel, TSMC, Samsung, Nvidia, AMD, ASML, AMAT, LRCX, TEL, KLAC.
The trap: these are massive companies. Even if SpaceX is a customer, SpaceX probably won't move the needle much unless TeraFab becomes real and named contracts emerge.

#### 8. Satellite power, batteries, thermal, and optical links

SpaceX uses solar arrays and optical inter-satellite laser links on Starlink satellites, and SpaceX has started selling its own satellite laser-link technology externally. That makes outside laser suppliers less obvious; SpaceX appears very in-house here.

Public proxies only: Rocket Lab/SolAero, KULR, Amprius, Coherent, Lumentum, MKS.
I would not score these highly as SpaceX suppliers without direct evidence. They're thematic space/optics/power names, not proven SpaceX bottlenecks.

### The highest-quality public SpaceX supplier basket to research first

If I were building a real SpaceX public-supplier diligence list, I'd start here:

STMicroelectronics — confirmed, huge-volume Starlink silicon supplier.
Filtronic — best small-cap pure-play Starlink RF bottleneck.
Linde — propellant infrastructure and launch-rate bottleneck.
Wistron NeWeb — Starlink routers/network gear manufacturing.
Chin-Poon — PCB/electronics supplier with confirmed SpaceX exposure.
Universal Microwave — RF/microwave satellite-component exposure.
Shenmao — solder/PCB materials, smaller and more obscure.
Velo3D — real SpaceX tech link, but equity risk is high.
EchoStar — strategic direct-to-cell spectrum asset, not classic supplier.
Compeq / Tong Hsing / Meiko / Kinpo — lower-confidence Asia proxy basket.

### Names I would not count as SpaceX suppliers without more proof

Redwire, Sidus, Planet Labs, Rocket Lab, KULR, Amprius, AST SpaceMobile, Intuitive Machines are often in the "space trade" bucket, but many are customers, launch partners, competitors, or thematic neighbors rather than SpaceX supply-chain bottlenecks. Sidus and Redwire, for example, show up around SpaceX launches/payloads, not as core suppliers.

### Bottom line

The real public SpaceX bottleneck list is much narrower than the meme list. The highest-signal names are:

STM, Filtronic, Linde, Wistron NeWeb, Chin-Poon, Universal Microwave, Shenmao, Velo3D, EchoStar, T-Mobile.

The asymmetric hunting ground is not STM or Linde. Those are quality but diluted by size. The possible alpha is in the smaller confirmed/publicly named suppliers: Filtronic, Universal Microwave, Chin-Poon, Wistron NeWeb, Shenmao, and maybe Velo3D if the balance sheet doesn't turn into a tire fire.

I can monitor new SpaceX supplier disclosures, filings, RNS releases, Taiwan exchange updates, and Reuters reports weekly and alert you when a new public bottleneck name surfaces.

### Layer-by-layer bottleneck map

#### 1. xAI compute: GPUs, racks, networking, power

This is the strongest current bottleneck cluster. Nvidia, Supermicro, Dell, Solaris Energy Infrastructure, Oracle, Microsoft, Caterpillar/Solar Turbines, and Apollo are the public names with the most direct xAI relevance. Nvidia is the non-negotiable heart of the cluster; Supermicro/Dell are rack infrastructure; Solaris and CAT/Solar Turbines are the "power now, grid later" bridge; Oracle/Microsoft are Grok distribution/hosting; Apollo is compute-financing infrastructure.

Best asymmetric candidates in this layer:
SEI is the most interesting smaller public name because power is the thing everyone underestimates until the data center is sitting there with no electrons. SMCI and DELL are cleaner xAI hardware suppliers, but much larger and more broadly followed. APO is a financial bottleneck, not an operating bottleneck.

#### 2. SpaceX / Starlink: RF, satellite electronics, terminal hardware, launch consumables

The cleanest confirmed SpaceX/Starlink suppliers are STMicroelectronics, Filtronic, Wistron NeWeb, Chin-Poon, Universal Microwave, Shenmao, Linde, and Velo3D. ST is the volume monster. Filtronic is the best small-cap RF bottleneck. The Taiwan cluster is the "Starlink hardware supply chain" basket. Linde is the launch-rate gas bottleneck.

Best asymmetric candidates in this layer:
Filtronic, Universal Microwave, Chin-Poon, Wistron NeWeb, Shenmao. That's the real hunting ground. STM is important, but Elon exposure is diluted inside a much bigger semi business.

#### 3. Tesla AI / FSD / Optimus: chips, cameras, sensors, manufacturing automation

The clean public names here are Samsung Electronics, TSMC, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Luminar, Onsemi, LK Technology/IDRA. Samsung and TSMC are the AI-chip bottlenecks. Samsung Electro-Mechanics is the camera-module candidate. Luminar is a testing/calibration LiDAR relationship, not production FSD. Onsemi has teardown evidence for older Tesla camera sensors. IDRA/LK is manufacturing-process leverage through gigapress systems.

Best asymmetric candidates in this layer:
Samsung Electro-Mechanics if camera-module exposure is real/current; LK Technology if Tesla-style gigacasting spreads further; Luminar only as a speculative sensor/testing read-through, not a "Tesla adopts LiDAR" thesis. Don't get high on your own supply-chain fumes there.

#### 4. Tesla Energy / EV batteries: cells, graphite, lithium, nickel

The best confirmed public names are LG Energy Solution, Panasonic, CATL, Syrah, Talon, BHP, Vale, Liontown, BYD, Glencore, Ganfeng, Yahua. LGES is the cleanest current Tesla Energy supply win because the U.S. government confirmed the Megapack 3 LFP deal. Syrah is the most interesting non-China graphite bottleneck, but execution risk is high. Talon is a future U.S. nickel option. Liontown is cleaner lithium exposure.

Best asymmetric candidates in this layer:
Syrah, Talon, Liontown. These are higher-risk, smaller, and more dependent on project execution / commodity cycles. LGES, Panasonic, CATL, BHP, Vale are real suppliers, but not pure Elon upside vehicles.

### Neocloud answer

For Elon-specific public-company supplier exposure, I would not rank CoreWeave, Nebius, or Crusoe as confirmed suppliers right now. xAI's strategy appears more vertically controlled: build Colossus, finance chips, buy/rent power, and use Oracle/Microsoft for model hosting/distribution. Oracle is the real public cloud name with a confirmed xAI relationship; Microsoft is a hosting/distribution relationship; CoreWeave/Nebius are broader AI infra comps, not confirmed Elon-stack bottlenecks.

### Investment lens

Bias: Bullish on the Elon-stack bottleneck theme, but selective. The best current bottleneck clusters are xAI compute/power, Starlink RF/electronics, and Tesla battery materials/cells.

Macro overlay: AI infrastructure remains the hotter table than generic EV supply. That means NVDA / SMCI / DELL / SEI / ORCL / Starlink RF names deserve more urgency than lithium/cobalt names, where commodity oversupply can suffocate otherwise good supplier stories.

Technical summary: Not running chart levels here because this is a supplier map, not a trade entry sheet. For any ticker from this list, I'd require Stage 2 or early Stage 2 continuation behavior, above key moving averages, volume confirmation, and a defined stop. No stop = no trade.

Best setup candidates: Filtronic, SEI, Universal Microwave, Chin-Poon, Wistron NeWeb, Shenmao, Syrah, Talon, Liontown.

Invalidation: Supplier relationship ends, contract is too small to move revenue, customer concentration becomes a balance-sheet risk, dilution accelerates, or the chart breaks into Stage 4.

Social sentiment overlay: SpaceX/xAI/Tesla supplier chatter is heating up, especially after SpaceX IPO-proxy speculation in Asia. That increases discovery upside, but also crowding risk in thin names. Reuters specifically noted investors hunting SpaceX proxy plays in Asia.

Trade grade for the theme: A- theme, but individual tickers vary wildly. Filtronic / SEI are the most interesting from a bottleneck-purity standpoint. Syrah / Talon / Liontown are asymmetric but commodity-cycle exposed. Luminar / L&F are relationship-confirmed but thesis-risky.

Position size: For small confirmed bottleneck names, max 0.5%–1.0% portfolio risk per trade until chart + fundamentals confirm. Bigger liquid names can be sized higher, but the upside is less pure.

Portfolio risk check: Do not let this become "everything Elon touches." That's not a portfolio; that's a shrine. Keep correlation under control across AI power, Starlink RF, battery materials, and semis.

Biggest risk to thesis: Supplier exposure can sound huge and still be financially tiny. The key diligence question is not "Do they supply Elon?" It is: What percent of revenue / backlog / margin comes from Elon companies, and is that exposure expanding or peaking?
